Two generations of satellites, one record that did not add up.

MODIS has watched since 2000, VIIRS since 2012. Over south-east Australia in 2019 they counted 50,497 and 631,751 hotspots: 12.5 times apart.

firecal makes them agree.

VIIRS is mapped onto MODIS tile by tile, fitted on the years both saw. On 2019, a year the fit never saw, the gap falls from 12.5 times to 0.77.

The burning calendar.

Months across, years down: south-east Australia from November 2000 to December 2019, from NASA FIRMS. The darker the cell, the more hotspots.

December 2019: 7,112 hotspots, ± 88.

A median December here has 7. The month rises past its uncertainty and past every year before it, so it carries the diamond: unusual, and not a change of sensor.

The years most like 2019.

2009, 2001 and 2002, compared by rank within each month, so a quiet place and a busy one are judged the same way. History, not a forecast.

Every number says how sure it is.

Each dot is a month of 2019, each black tick its median over the years before, each blue line its uncertainty at two sigma. Most months sit near their past.

Unusual means above both.

A month is flagged only when it rises past its band and past every year before it. In 2019 that is November and December.

Pin a region before you go.

firecal is a static site. Pinning keeps the tiles of a region in the browser: 4.21 MiB for this one.

Then lose the signal.

Draw a new area and it still answers, in under a millisecond: the same Rust core that harmonized twenty years of satellites runs in the browser.

One core, built to not fall over.

The science is one Rust core. On a laptop it ingests and harmonizes twenty years of FIRMS; compiled to WebAssembly, the same code answers in your browser. There is no server: the site is static files.

It agrees with itself.

The browser build and the laptop build give identical bytes on 8,968 test calls, and every change is checked on macOS, Windows and Linux before it is merged.

Thirty seconds.

What it does not claim.

  • A hotspot is not a fire.It is a thermal anomaly seen from orbit: not a fire, and not a burned area.
  • Harmonized, not identical.VIIRS counts are mapped to MODIS-equivalent counts, fitted on the years both sensors saw.
  • The uncertainty is an estimate.It adds cell errors as if they were independent, which is optimistic where neighbours share a bias.
  • History, not a forecast.The years most like this one say what happened before, not what will happen next.
  • The newest months are provisional.They come from near-real-time data until the standard product replaces it.

What comes next.

  • Uruguay, our home.Its archive is cut at the border, so the harmonizer needs a regional mode before Uruguay can have its calendar.
  • Every tile of the world.The tiles are the same 10° grid everywhere; what is missing is the full FIRMS archive, behind a NASA Earthdata login.
  • The phone in the truck.It is one static page, so it should open on a phone with no signal; we have not measured it on one yet.
